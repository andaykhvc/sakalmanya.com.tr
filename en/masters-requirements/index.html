Master’s in Germany: Requirements 2026

Degree recognition, subject fit, grade average, language certificates, the uni-assist and VPD route, deadlines, fees, visa funding and DAAD scholarships — every topic you meet on the way to a master’s in Germany, on one page, with sources.

In short: what does a master’s in Germany require?

A master’s in Germany generally requires a recognised bachelor’s degree, an academic background that fits the programme you are applying to, the grade level the university expects, and a language certificate in the programme’s language of instruction. There is, however, no single grade average, language level or document list that applies across Germany. Individual programmes may additionally require specific bachelor modules and ECTS credits, a portfolio, GRE/GMAT, work experience, reference letters, a motivation letter or a uni-assist VPD. The binding requirements are always in the current admission regulations of the programme itself.

Core requirements for a master’s in Germany

Holding a bachelor’s degree does not by itself entitle you to apply to every master’s programme. German universities generally look at: a recognised bachelor’s or equivalent first academic degree, the academic fit between your bachelor’s field and the programme, the modules and ECTS credits considered necessary, the grade level the university expects, language proficiency in the programme’s language of instruction, and any programme-specific extras. What decides is always the university’s own admission regulations; there is no general “German standard”.

Master’s programmes usually fall into two groups. Consecutive master’s programmes follow directly on from a bachelor’s degree; here the matching bachelor’s field and specific academic content generally matter. Weiterbildender (continuing education) master’s programmes usually expect a period of professional experience alongside the degree and may charge tuition. Which group a programme belongs to affects a great deal, from the language requirement to the cost.

Degree and university recognition

anabin is the official information portal used to assess where foreign institutions and qualifications sit within the German education system. Universities can be listed there with statuses such as H+, H+/- or H-; H+ is an important positive signal. An anabin entry alone does not guarantee admission to a particular master’s programme, though — academic eligibility is always decided by the university, which may request an additional degree assessment or further documents.

You cannot assume that every bachelor’s degree from Turkey automatically suits every German master’s programme; the institution’s anabin status and the programme’s own admission criteria have to be read together.

Subject fit and ECTS requirements

Universities do not only look at the name of your degree; your transcript and module contents may also be examined. Specific ECTS requirements can apply in mathematics, statistics, programming, economics, research methods or the core subjects of the field. Missing ECTS can lead to rejection; some universities allow a limited number of missing modules to be completed later, but that is not a general right and varies by programme.

Module descriptions or a module handbook may be requested during the application. A cross-field master’s application always has to be assessed programme by programme. A data-science master’s, for instance, may require a certain volume of mathematics, statistics and programming credits alongside the degree — that is an example, not a general rule, and each programme publishes its own conditions on its own page.

Grade average for a master’s in Germany

There is no Germany-wide grade requirement for a master’s. Some programmes ask for 2.5, 2.3, 2.0 or better on the German grading scale, while others set only a formal minimum. Some use a ranking, an NC (Numerus Clausus), an aptitude assessment or an interview; others weigh required modules and ECTS fit more heavily than the grade itself. NC-frei does not mean “unconditional admission”: in an NC-frei programme you still have to meet every academic, language and document requirement separately.

For converting a Turkish grade into the German scale, either uni-assist’s own method or the university’s own assessment may apply. The “Modified Bavarian Formula” is a commonly encountered method, but a calculation you run yourself online does not replace the official assessment; the binding conversion is done by uni-assist or the university.

Can you do a master’s in Germany with a 2.50 GPA?

Yes, it can be possible on some programmes. A 2.50 in the Turkish system means neither acceptance nor rejection on its own; the German grade conversion, subject fit, ECTS requirements and the competitiveness of the programme are assessed together. The current admission criteria of your target university and programme should always be checked separately.

German and English language requirements

German-taught master’s programmes

For German-taught programmes, accepted certificates can include TestDaF, DSH, telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule, the Goethe-Zertifikat, ÖSD and other certificates the university recognises separately. Not every certificate and not every level is automatically accepted everywhere. According to DAAD’s general information, many German-taught programmes may require at least B2, but master’s programmes frequently set higher and exam-specific conditions. It cannot be said that B2 is enough for all German-taught master’s programmes.

English-taught master’s programmes

English-taught programmes may require IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT or Cambridge certificates. Some accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter showing your bachelor’s was taught in English as an alternative; not every university accepts an MOI, and the minimum score varies by programme. Academic admission to English-taught programmes usually does not require a German certificate, though German still helps with daily life, internships and finding work.

Can you apply without IELTS?

Some programmes accept TOEFL, Cambridge certificates or an MOI letter as an alternative; this depends entirely on the programme’s current language regulations and does not apply at every university.

Documents required for a master’s application

It varies by programme, but an application file usually contains: your bachelor’s degree certificate or a provisional graduation document, a full transcript, a diploma supplement if you have one, module descriptions, your school leaving certificate and earlier educational records where needed, your passport, CV, motivation letter and language certificate, plus — on request — reference letters, proof of work experience, a portfolio, GRE or GMAT results, a uni-assist VPD, any extra forms the university asks for, and translations or certifications. This list is not the same for every programme; each university publishes its current list on its own application page.

Translation note: for translations prepared in Turkey, uni-assist requires the sworn translator’s stamp to carry the Turkish wording “yeminli tercüman”; a stamp only in English or German may not always be accepted. This is not a universal rule for all universities but uni-assist’s own rule for documents from Turkey. View the official source ↗

What are uni-assist and the VPD?

Not every university uses uni-assist. Some run the entire application process through it, while others only require a VPD (Vorprüfungsdokumentation). A VPD is a preliminary assessment of the educational documents submitted, of your eligibility to apply and of the German grade conversion — it is not an admission letter. After receiving a VPD you generally still have to apply to your chosen university separately; the VPD is issued for the university you select.

According to current uni-assist information, the VPD procedure usually takes 4 to 6 weeks once the documents are complete and can be valid for one year from the date of issue. The direct application also has to be completed before the university’s deadline, which is why the VPD request should not be left to the final weeks.

Application deadlines

There is no single deadline covering all master’s programmes in Germany. Winter semester applications are collected in spring and summer for many programmes, while summer semester applications mostly fall in autumn and winter. Dates such as 15 July and 15 January are not fixed or universal, though; earlier deadlines can apply to international students, to applicants who need a visa, and to particular master’s programmes. Because the VPD and document assessment take time, it is sensible to start months in advance. The deadline should always be checked on the university’s official programme page; for keeping track, the post How do you keep track of German application deadlines? may also help.

Master’s fees in Germany

At public universities in Germany, most consecutive master’s programmes carry no general tuition fee — but that does not make them “completely free”. A semester contribution known as the Semesterbeitrag is payable, and it can cover student services, administrative costs and sometimes a transport pass. Private universities and some continuing-education master’s programmes may charge substantial fees.

In Baden-Württemberg, tuition of €1,500 per semester may apply at public universities for many students from outside the EU/EEA, with certain exemptions available. Universities in Bavaria may also set fees for non-EU/EEA students on a programme-by-programme and university-by-university basis. Each university’s current fee page should be checked separately.

Visa and funding requirements

University admission does not automatically guarantee a student visa; the appropriate visa type has to be determined separately depending on your admission or application status. A visa application generally calls for proof of funding, health insurance, a passport, admission or application documents, language proficiency where required, and any other educational records the consulate asks for.

According to official information from Make it in Germany, the minimum amount to be shown in a blocked account for 2026 is published as €11,904 per year (€992 available per month). Because different monthly figures can appear on individual official visa pages, the guidance that matters more than any fixed number is this: funding amounts are updated, so the current figure must be checked with the competent German mission abroad and on the official government page before applying. View the official source ↗

Alternative routes to proving funding can include a recognised scholarship, a Verpflichtungserklärung (formal obligation letter) or other proof the consulate accepts, depending on the case. Do not assume these will be accepted automatically in every application; for detailed visa preparation, see the German student visa guide.

A master’s in Germany with a DAAD scholarship

“The DAAD scholarship” is not one standard scholarship

The Deutscher Akademischer Austauschdienst (DAAD) runs many scholarship programmes for different countries, academic fields, applicant profiles and goals. Conditions, deadlines, funding amounts and document lists all change with the specific call. To find a suitable one, use the DAAD Scholarship Database ↗.

What is generally assessed

It varies by programme, but DAAD master’s scholarships generally consider: a recognised first academic degree, academic performance, the quality and course record of your bachelor’s, proficiency in the language of instruction, academic and personal motivation, the logic behind your choice of programme in Germany, how the study plan connects to your career goals, internships and work experience, social or voluntary activity, extracurricular skills, and the scope to take equal opportunity and particular life circumstances into account. There is no single minimum grade average across all DAAD scholarships.

Example: Master Studies for All Academic Disciplines

This programme is given as an example of how much DAAD scholarships can differ and should not be generalised to all DAAD programmes. According to the general information in the current call: the applicant must have completed their first academic degree by the time the funding period begins; the most recent academic degree should as a rule be no more than six years old at the time of application; academic performance, language or working-language proficiency and academic and personal motivation are assessed; the future contribution of studying in Germany, internships, work experience and extracurricular activities may be taken into account; and the documents usually include a tabular CV, a letter of motivation, degree certificate/transcript and a language certificate. The binding document list and dates must be checked in the current call each year. View the official call ↗

EPOS scholarships

EPOS is a separate DAAD programme family aimed at particular applicants from developing countries for selected development-related master’s programmes. Alongside a bachelor’s degree, at least two years of relevant professional experience is usually expected. Not every master’s programme falls under EPOS; whether Turkey and your own profile currently qualify has to be checked separately in the call for the chosen programme. Do not assume that two years of work experience is required for all DAAD scholarships — that condition is tied to specific programmes such as EPOS. View EPOS scholarships ↗

The scholarship and the university application are separate processes

Applying for a DAAD scholarship is not the same as applying to a university. A scholarship application does not secure admission; the academic decision is made by the university itself. Some scholarship programmes ask you to name a programme or a university link when you apply. University and scholarship deadlines can differ, and some university applications may have to be completed before the scholarship result is announced. A DAAD award is only valid within the rules of the relevant programme and alongside university admission.

What a DAAD scholarship can cover

Depending on the programme, some DAAD scholarships can include: a monthly payment, a travel allowance, contributions to health, accident and liability insurance, a rent subsidy, family allowances, and support for a language course or for study and research costs. Not every item appears in every programme. DAAD does not automatically cover university tuition fees; if you are considering an expensive private programme, check this specifically in the call. For exact amounts, always consult the relevant programme call.

Does a DAAD scholarship replace a blocked account?

A valid official scholarship document showing a sufficient amount can be accepted as proof of funding for a student visa. If the scholarship amount falls below the required funding level, additional proof may be requested. The final assessment rests with the competent German mission abroad.

What grade average do you need for a DAAD scholarship?

There is no single minimum grade average across all DAAD master’s scholarships. Academic performance matters, but motivation, choice of programme, language ability, experience and the overall profile are assessed together. The current call for each scholarship should be checked separately.

Applying for a German master’s step by step

  1. Decide on your field and language of instruction. Whether you are aiming at German or English shapes every step that follows.
  2. Find programmes through DAAD or university databases. Compare admission regulations, not programme names.
  3. Read each programme’s admission regulations. Grade, language and ECTS requirements differ from programme to programme.
  4. Check subject fit and ECTS against your transcript. Spotting missing credits early matters.
  5. Confirm the language requirement and accepted certificates. Check which exam is accepted at which score.
  6. Research your institution’s recognition on anabin. Find out the status of the university you graduated from.
  7. Prepare documents, translations and certifications. Plan the sworn translation rules early.
  8. Check whether uni-assist or a VPD is required. The procedure can take several weeks.
  9. Complete the application before the deadline. Receiving a VPD does not mean the application is finished.
  10. After admission, start funding, insurance and visa steps. Prepare the blocked account or scholarship document in time.
  11. Research suitable DAAD and other scholarships as separate processes. Track the scholarship calendar independently of the university one.

Checklist for applicants from Turkey

  • Fit between your bachelor’s field and the target programme
  • Required ECTS and modules on the transcript
  • Degree certificate and provisional graduation document

  • Sworn translation requirements
  • Whether an apostille or certification is needed — this varies by university and application system
  • uni-assist country notes

When applying from Turkey to a German master’s, do not assume the APS certificate is a general requirement; the APS condition is tied to particular countries and procedures. Applicants from Turkey should check the official document list of the programme itself.

Frequently asked questions

What grade average do you need for a master’s in Germany?

There is no single minimum grade average that applies to every master’s programme in Germany. The requirement differs from programme to programme: some set a formal minimum, others use a ranking or an aptitude assessment. The exact figure is stated in the current admission regulations of the programme you are applying to.

Can you do a master’s in Germany with a 2.50 GPA?

Yes, it can be possible on some programmes. A 2.50 in the Turkish system means neither acceptance nor rejection on its own; the German grade conversion, subject fit, ECTS requirements and the competition in that round are assessed together. Some programmes weigh module content and motivation more heavily than the grade.

Do you need German for a master’s in Germany?

Only for programmes taught in German. English-taught master’s programmes generally do not require a German certificate for academic admission, though German still helps considerably with daily life, internships and finding work.

Is IELTS mandatory for an English-taught master’s?

Some programmes accept TOEFL, Cambridge certificates or a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter as an alternative. This depends entirely on the programme’s current language regulations, and not every university accepts an MOI.

Can you do a master’s in a different field from your bachelor’s?

It can be possible on some programmes, but the university will examine your transcript and module content and may require specific ECTS credits. A cross-field application always has to be assessed programme by programme; there is no general rule.

What does NC-frei mean for a master’s programme?

NC-frei means the programme does not apply a numerical grade restriction (Numerus Clausus); it does not mean unconditional admission. In an NC-frei programme you still have to meet every academic, language and document requirement separately.

Is a master’s in Germany free?

Most consecutive master’s programmes at public universities charge no general tuition fee, but that does not make the degree free. A semester contribution is payable, and some federal states and programmes may charge tuition on top.

What is the difference between uni-assist and a VPD?

uni-assist is an organisation through which some universities run the entire application process. A VPD (Vorprüfungsdokumentation) is only a preliminary review of your educational documents and grade conversion; it is not an admission letter, and you usually still have to apply to the university separately.

Does receiving a VPD mean I have been admitted?

No. A VPD is only a preliminary review of documents and does not replace an admission decision. After receiving it, you still have to apply to your chosen university separately and before the application deadline.

When do master’s applications in Germany take place?

There is no single deadline covering all master’s programmes in Germany. Winter semester applications are mostly collected in spring and summer, and summer semester applications mostly in autumn and winter; some programmes set earlier dates for international applicants. The exact deadline should be checked on the university’s official page.

What grade average do you need for a DAAD scholarship?

There is no single minimum grade average covering all DAAD master’s scholarships. Academic performance is an important criterion, but motivation, choice of programme, language ability, experience and the overall profile are assessed together. The current call for each scholarship should be checked separately.

Is a DAAD scholarship hard to get?

DAAD scholarships are competitive and vary in difficulty from programme to programme. Application numbers, available places and applicant profiles change from year to year. No reliable success rate can be quoted; an application should be backed by a strong academic and personal profile.

Does a DAAD scholarship guarantee university admission?

No. Applying for a DAAD scholarship and applying to a university are separate processes. A scholarship application does not secure admission; the academic decision is always made by the university itself.

Does a DAAD scholarship replace a blocked account?

A valid official scholarship document showing a sufficient amount can be accepted as proof of funding for a student visa. If the scholarship amount falls below the required funding level, additional proof may be requested. The final assessment rests with the competent German mission abroad.

Do you apply to DAAD and to the university separately?

Generally yes. A DAAD scholarship application and a university application can have different calendars and different document lists. Some scholarship programmes ask you to name a programme when you apply, but admission always comes separately from the university itself.

Do you need work experience for a DAAD scholarship?

This is not a rule for all DAAD scholarships. A specific work experience requirement is tied above all to development-focused programmes such as EPOS, where at least two years of relevant experience is usually expected. Most academically focused scholarships do not have this condition.

Is a Turkish bachelor’s degree valid in Germany?

Where an institution and a degree sit within the German education system can be checked in the anabin database. An anabin entry alone does not guarantee admission to a particular master’s programme, though; academic eligibility is decided by the university.

Is a YKS document required for a master’s?

Master’s admission rests primarily on the bachelor’s degree. Even so, uni-assist or the university may request earlier educational records such as a school leaving certificate or university entrance document in order to verify your educational history; this varies by programme.

Is a school leaving certificate required for a master’s application?

Some universities or uni-assist include the school leaving certificate in the document list in order to assess the completeness of your educational history. This is not compulsory in every programme and should be checked against the current list of the relevant application system.

Can you apply for a master’s before graduating?

Some universities allow final-year students who have completed a certain number of credits to submit a provisional or conditional application. This is not a general right; the binding condition depends on the current admission regulations of the university and the programme.
